# split-canvas

## Look

A warm product-UI canvas above a continuous presenter window, rounded cards, accent rules and compact caption pills.

## Best for

Explainers and tutorials where the presenter stays on screen and the upper canvas shows one idea at a time.

## Layouts and footage

`split` (graphics above the seam, presenter below) for most beats; `takeover` for a full-frame idea; `presenter` when the face alone carries the line.

## Palette and brand

- **Brand colours:** one ground (the profile background, or the style's warm paper) with ink text; the highlight colour alternates between primary and accent from scene to scene; captions become a light card pill with ink text.

## Recipe

**Direction:** demonstrate while the speaker explains

- **Decision:** Choose it when seeing the speaker and a demonstration together builds understanding. A personal line returns to `presenter`; a detailed diagram or result can use `takeover` if the split would make it small.
- **Composition/material:** Give the upper product canvas one dominant card or relationship. Keep rounded UI surfaces, sans type and a small accent rule; subordinate supporting text. The presenter window and caption pill must remain distinct and clear. Use the supported profile palette rules in [STYLES.md](../STYLES.md#brand-colours).
- **Timing/action:** Cue the meaningful UI state or diagram change on the reviewed phrase. Land it, demonstrate its cause, then hold the result. Keep the same UI/object through related steps rather than restarting with a title on every line.
- **Transition/sound:** Match the object's position across beats or use a clear state change. A click marks a real selected control; a soft landing can support a card arrival. Protect speech and give a personal line room. Effects follow [SOUND.md](../SOUND.md).
- **Application/review:** Use `process`, `proof`, `screen-focus` or another validated split family when they fit. Check that the viewer can read the demo without a zoom that softens source text. If the upper canvas only repeats the subtitle, give it an explanatory job.

### Decision order

1. Layout timeline first, by what each sentence does: a demonstrable claim → `split` with its visual above the seam; a detailed diagram, number or result the split would shrink → `takeover`; a hook, personal line, direct address or CTA → `presenter`. The presenter stays on screen for most of the runtime; a long run of takeovers has left the style, so redistribute.
2. Frame 0 is already moving: a caption pill and an animating graphic are live; no title card, no fade-in, no dead beat.
3. Walk the words into element entrances: about one spoken word or short phrase = one new element on the canvas. Ask of each claim "what artifact proves this?": number → rolling counter with the real figure; name → typed reveal; verdict → stamped badge; mechanism → stepwise diagram; product state → mock UI filling row by row. Never invent a number to fill a counter.
4. Caption every word and put the pill where the layout says: on the seam in `split`, low-centre in `takeover`, chest level in `presenter`.
5. Dress every boundary and vary the dressing; no naked cut between scenes.
6. Sound last, on real state changes only.

## Style-owned scenes

No scene family is reserved for this style. It renders the shared families recorded in [MOTION-CHOICES.md](../MOTION-CHOICES.md).

## Avoid

- If the upper canvas only repeats the subtitle, give it an explanatory job.
- Keep the same UI/object through related steps rather than restarting with a title on every line.
- If the presenter window crops the face, or the upper canvas only repeats the subtitle for a whole section, you have stopped applying this style.
