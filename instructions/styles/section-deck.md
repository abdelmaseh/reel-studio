# section-deck

## Look

Dark UI sections with a kicker, an accent headline, semantic chips and held clause captions.

## Best for

Explainers, tutorials and comparisons that fall into clear sections.

## Layouts and footage

`split` for sections under a continuous presenter; `takeover` for comparisons, processes and data.

## Palette and brand

- **Brand colours:** sections cycle through three grounds — the profile background (or the deck's navy), a deep primary and a deep accent — each with readable ink and its own highlight; captions stay a solid pill.

## Recipe

**Direction:** make the chapter structure visible

- **Decision:** Choose it when the argument naturally divides into sections, stages or comparison criteria. Define each section's question and answer before deciding how many scenes it needs.
- **Composition/material:** Use a dark section ground, a concise kicker, an accent headline and semantic chips. Keep the main answer visually stronger than the section label. Palette cycling follows the supported deck rules, not a different color for each sentence.
- **Timing/action:** Establish a section, reveal the relationship that explains it, and hold the answer. Stagger items in reading or causal order. A short spoken aside may keep the existing section without a new reveal.
- **Transition/sound:** Carry the section's selected item or direction into the next state. A chapter change can reset the ground after the prior thought resolves. Use restrained ticks for real stage changes; avoid a sting for every chip. Effects follow [SOUND.md](../SOUND.md).
- **Application/review:** `process`, `comparison` and the appropriate semantic families can structure the material; check their actual layout support. Review whether the headings explain a progression rather than creating unrelated slide cards.

### Decision order

1. Sections first: 8 to 13 for 36–60 s, each one idea and one clause cluster (about 2.5–5 s). Prove the arc twice: problem hook → proof → mechanism or surprise → framework or numbered list → payoff → comment gate → follow outro.
2. Choose one cadence and hold it: a metronomic deck (uniform swaps, one pill per section) or split ↔ takeover alternation (a section spans several clauses: intro card → demo → punchline).
3. One static pill per clause: four to nine words that compress the clause, held for its whole length; no karaoke, no word swaps, no highlight fills.
4. Hero set-piece by artifact: number → rolling counter or ring gauge; process → rows populating with checks; contrast → without/with cards; product state → a mock editor or browser that fills in; verdict → stamped badge. Numbers always animate and are always real.
5. Whole-canvas swaps only, always dressed; the voice chains across the boundary, never pausing at a swap. Inside a section, two to four animated events per three seconds; no five-second window without an event.
6. Sound on the landing of each swap and set-piece; silence under the follow hold and the goodbye.

## Style-owned scenes

No scene family is reserved for this style. It renders the shared families recorded in [MOTION-CHOICES.md](../MOTION-CHOICES.md).

## Avoid

- Palette cycling follows the supported deck rules, not a different color for each sentence.
- Use restrained ticks for real stage changes; avoid a sting for every chip.
- Review whether the headings explain a progression rather than creating unrelated slide cards.
- If you are swapping pills at word rate, cropping the presenter's face in a split, or fading a section instead of swapping it, you have stopped applying this style.
